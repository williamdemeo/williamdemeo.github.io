# agda-algebras

A library of universal algebra in [Agda](https://github.com/agda/agda) — algebras,
homomorphisms, congruences, terms, varieties, and the equational logic underneath
them — built around a fully constructive, machine-checked proof of Birkhoff's HSP
theorem in Martin-Löf type theory, joint with Jacques Carette. The hard part was
not the theorem, which has been textbook material since 1935; it was that a
machine will not accept the textbook proof. Birkhoff's argument quantifies over
*all* algebras in a class and then builds a free algebra over the carrier of one
of them, and in a predicative type theory those two moves do not live at the same
universe level — so the formal proof has to say, explicitly and everywhere, at
which level each object sits and how to lift between them. The proof, the
library, and the numbers below are all checkable: the theorem type-checks against
Agda 2.8.0 in CI, and the paper is published with a DOI.

## What a machine-checked Birkhoff proof needs that a textbook proof does not

Birkhoff's theorem says a class of algebras is a *variety* — closed under
homomorphic images, subalgebras and products — exactly when it is an *equational
class*, the class of all algebras satisfying some set of identities. Textbooks
state it as an "if and only if" and prove the hard direction in a page.

In the library the hard direction is one type:

```agda
Birkhoff : {𝑨 : Algebra a a} → 𝑨 ∈ Mod (Th (V ℓ ι 𝒦)) → 𝑨 ∈ V ℓ ι 𝒦
```

Read it right to left: if `𝑨` satisfies (`Mod`) every identity true of (`Th`) the
variety generated by `𝒦`, then `𝑨` is already in that variety. That is the
textbook inclusion `Mod (Th (V 𝒦)) ⊆ V 𝒦`, and three things about the formal
statement are invisible in the prose one.

**The universe levels are part of the theorem.** The private abbreviations
`a = α ⊔ ρᵃ ⊔ ℓ` and `ι = ov a` are the levels the proof operates at: `α` is the
carrier level of the generating class, `ρᵃ` the level of its equivalence, `ℓ` the
level of variables. `𝑨` is pinned to `Algebra a a` so that its carrier can serve
as the generating set of the relatively free algebra `𝔽[ ∣A∣ ]` — and that free
algebra does not fit alongside `𝑨`. It lives one universe up, at `ι = ov a`. A
predicative theory will not let you build a free algebra over a class and keep it
at the class's own level, so the proof factors through an explicit lift,
`Lift-Alg 𝑨 ι ι`, whose only job is to move `𝑨` up to where the free algebra
already is. Nothing in the textbook proof corresponds to that lift; it exists
because the ambient logic is predicative and the paper proof quietly is not.

**The "if and only if" is four separate facts.** `Birkhoff` above is one
inclusion. `Birkhoff-converse` is the other. Neither alone says "variety ⟺
equational class": that also needs the identity-preservation lemma `V-id1` — if
every algebra in `𝒦` satisfies an identity, then so does every algebra in the
variety `𝒦` generates, which yields `V 𝒦 ⊆ 𝒦` when `𝒦` is an equational class —
together with the expansiveness of `V` (`V-expa : 𝒦 ⊆ V 𝒦`). Only the four
together are the theorem. A prose proof gets to call two of those obvious.

**It is constructive, and that is a choice with a cost.** The proof postulates no
function extensionality. That is unusual — extensionality is the standard escape
hatch when two functions are provably equal at every argument but not
propositionally equal — and avoiding it is most of why the library is shaped the
way it is.

## Why setoids

The library carried two incompatible developments of the same vocabulary through
its 2.x line: `Base/`, which uses propositional equality on raw carrier types,
and `Setoid/`, in which an algebra is a set together with an equivalence relation
that its operations must respect. Both define `Algebra`, `Hom`, `Con`,
`Subalgebra`. Neither could import the other.

`Base/` is the easier read, and it is closer to how universal algebra is usually
presented in type theory. It was frozen anyway. The reasoning is recorded in the
library's own [ADR-001](https://github.com/ualib/agda-algebras/blob/master/docs/adr/001-setoid-as-canonical.md),
and it comes down to three properties `Base/` does not have:

- **Constructivity.** The setoid development does not postulate extensionality;
  carrying the equivalence explicitly is what removes the need for it.
- **Interoperability.** It matches the idiom of the Agda standard library's
  `Algebra.Bundles`, which is where anything that wants to talk to the wider
  ecosystem has to land.
- **A migration path.** Definitions phrased in terms of the algebra's own
  equivalence, rather than propositional equality, port to cubical Agda by
  replacing that equivalence with a path type. That makes an eventual cubical
  rewrite substantially mechanical rather than a second full development.

The cost is real and is worth stating plainly, because it is the part a reader
evaluating the decision should weigh. `Base/` is a breaking change for anyone who
depended on the pre-3.0 API. It is frozen under `Legacy/Base/` rather than
deleted — partly because several lemmas still exist only there, partly because
the TYPES 2021 citations point at `Base/`-tree URLs that should keep resolving —
which means CI type-checks both trees indefinitely. Of the 68 frozen modules, 35
have a canonical replacement, 29 are waiting on later milestones, and the
remainder are the extensionality machinery that the setoid formulation retires by
construction.

Two framings worth separating: the tension here is setoids versus propositional
equality on raw carriers, not setoids versus quotient types. Quotients were never
the alternative on the table, because the point of the exercise was to avoid the
axioms a quotient would have needed.

## Scale, and how to check it

The library's documentation site publishes its own size, and the figure is
generated at build time from the source tree rather than typed in: **302 literate
Agda modules and roughly 60,000 lines of Agda**, counting the canonical tree and
excluding the 68 frozen `Legacy/` modules.

That number is reproducible rather than asserted. It counts lines inside the
`agda` code fences of every `.lagda.md` file under `src/` outside `Legacy/`, and
it appears on the [documentation landing page](https://agda-algebras.universalalgebra.org),
regenerated on every docs build. Recomputing it directly from a clone at the
commit this page was written against gives 302 modules and 59,613 lines, which is
the same figure.

The library targets Agda 2.8.0 and standard library 2.3, both pinned by a Nix
flake, and CI type-checks the canonical tree, the frozen legacy tree, and the
research certificates on every push.

## What is next

Version 3.0 is a reconstruction rather than a release, and it is not finished.
The setoid tree is canonical and the legacy tree is frozen. The `Classical/`
layer — specific theories built on the universal-algebra foundation, each with a
Σ-typed core and a record-typed bundle view for standard-library interoperability
— is landing one structure at a time; magmas and semigroups are in, with monoids,
groups, lattices and rings following. A cubical development is the long-term
target for 4.0, and is currently a stub: designating it canonical today would be
aspirational rather than substantive.

The honest statement of the library's condition is the one its own ADR makes: the
setoid tree was declared canonical before it was self-sufficient, and several of
its modules still import basic definitions from the frozen tree. That is
type-correct and it is being unwound module by module, but it is the kind of
thing a reader deserves to hear from the author rather than discover in the
import graph.

The thread worth following from here is the [AI tooling](index.md) built against
this library: 60,000 lines of type-checked, human-written Agda with a published
theorem at the centre is a rare thing to have, and what it is rare *for* is
training and evaluating machine reasoning on formal mathematics.
