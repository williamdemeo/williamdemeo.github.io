<!-- more -->

(2021 Jan 20) I'm pleased to announce that on 12 January 2021 I achieved my goal of constructing a formal, machine-checked proof of Birkhoff's variety theorem in dependent type theory using the [Agda](https://wiki.portal.chalmers.se/agda/pmwiki.php) programming language.  

Birkhoff's celebrated theorem asserts that a class K of algebraic structures is closed under the taking of homomorphic images, subalgebras, and arbitrary products if and only if K is defined by a set of equations.

To the best of my knowledge, this is the first time Birkhoff's Theorem has been formulated and proved in dependent type theory and verified with a proof assistant.

The Agda Universal Algebra Library has undergone a makeover to make it more concise and elegant and to correct some flaws in the previous version.  The latest release is version 2.03.

The [main project webpage (ualib.org)](https://ualib.gitlab.io) is the best source for up-to-date documentation.

the [Agda Universal Algebra Library gitlab repository](https://gitlab.com/ualib/ualib.gitlab.io) is where one can download (clone or fork) and install the source code of the Agda UALib.

<section>
  <h2>Bounded Lattices and Fiber Products</h2>
  <div style="float: left">
  <a href = "https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08046"><img src = "/images/Bounded.png" height="150px" style="border:10px" /></a>
  </div> 
  <div style="float: right">
<a href="/images/demeo-ruskuk-mayr.jpg"><img src = "/images/demeo-ruskuk-mayr.jpg" height="150px" style="border:20px"/><div class="caption">DeMeo, Ruskuk, and Mayr in Boulder, 2018</div>
</a>
  </div> 
  In 2018 I began collaborating with <b>Peter Mayr</b> (CU Boulder) and <b>Nik Ruskuc</b> (University of St. Andrews) who were interested in knowing when a homomorphism $\varphi \colon \mathbf{F} \to \mathbf{L}$ from a finitely generated free lattice $\mathbf{F}$ onto a finite lattice $\mathbf L$ has a kernel $\ker \varphi$ that is a finitely generated sublattice of $\mathbf{F}^2$.  We conjectured that this could be characterized by whether or not the homomorphism is *bounded*. (See 
  <a href = "https://books.google.com/books?id=YdPxBwAAQBAJ&lpg=PR7&ots=N0yWMahcku&dq=free%20lattices%20freese&lr&pg=PR7#v=onepage&q=free%20lattices%20freese&f=false">the book by Freese, Jezek and Nation</a> for the definition of a bounded lattice homomorphism.) and I presented a proof of one direction of the conjecture at the <a href = "https://universalalgebra.github.io/ALH-2018/">Algebras and Lattices in Hawaii</a> conference in 2018.  By early 2019 we proved the converse and thus confirmed our conjecture.  Mayr and Ruskuc had in mind an application for the fact that our new result is equivalent to a characterization of <i>fiber products</i> of lattices.  A paper presenting our theorem characterizing bounded lattices was published in January of 2020 in the <i>International Journal of Algebra and Computation</i>, 30 (4):693--710, 2020. (<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08046">PREPRINT</a>)
</section>

<section>
<h2>Equational Logic and Complexity</h2>
  <div style="float: left">
  <a href = "https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07879"><img src = "/images/DFV-diffterms.png" height="150px" style="border:10px" /></a>
  </div> 
  <div style="float: right">
<a href="/images/DeMeoFreeseValeriote2018.jpg"><img src = "/images/DeMeoFreeseValeriote2018.jpg" height="150px" style="border:20px"/><div class="caption">DeMeo, Freese, and Valeriote in Hawaii, 2018</div>
</a>
  </div> 
Among my most significant research accomplishments is a discovery we made in 2018 that a certain decision problem about equations in varieties of algebraic structures, that previously seemed computationally out of reach is actually tractable. In collaboration with <b>Ralph Freese</b> (Univ Hawaii) and <b>Matthew Valeriote</b> (McMaster Univ), we considered the following practical question: Given a finite algebra $\mathbf{A}$ in a finite language, can we efficiently decide whether the variety generated by $\mathbf{A}$ has a so called <i>difference term</i>?  We answered this question (positively) in the idempotent case and then describe an algorithm for constructing difference term operations in polynomial-time. These discoveries were published in the <i>International Journal of Algebra and Computation</i>, 29 (6):927--949, 2019. (<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07879">PREPRINT</a>)
</section>

  <h2>Algebras and Algorithms, Structure and Complexity</h2>

  <div style="float: left">

In 2015, I joined a group of 8 other scientists to form a universal algebra research group and secure a 3-year NSF grant for the project, <i><a href="http://math.colorado.edu/algebra2016/">Algebras and Algorithms, Structure and Complexity Theory</a></i>. Our focus is on fundamental problems at the confluence of logic, algebra, and computer science, and our main goal is to deepen understanding of how to determine the complexity of certain types of computational problems.  We focus primarily on classes of algebraic problems whose solutions yield new information about the complexity of CSPs. These include scheduling problems, resource allocation problems, and problems reducible to solving systems of linear equations. CSPs are theoretically solvable, but some are not solvable efficiently.  Our work provides procedures for deciding whether a given instance of a CSP is tractable or intractable, and we develop efficient algorithms for finding solutions in the tractable cases.

This was recently accepted for publication in the journal <i>Logical Methods in Computer Science</i> (LMCS) (<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02867">PREPRINT</a>)
