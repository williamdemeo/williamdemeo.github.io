## 1998 April 03

**Instructions.** Do at least four problems in Part A, and at least two problems in Part B.

### PART A

1. Let $\\{x_n\\}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a bounded sequence of real numbers, and for each positive $n$ define $\hat{x}\_n = sup \_{k \geq n} x\_k$.\
   a. Explain why the limit $\ell = \lim_n \hat{x}_n$ exists.\
   b. Prove that, for any $\epsilon > 0$ and positive integer $N$, there exists an integer $k$ such that $k\geq N$ and $|x_k - \ell| < \epsilon$.

2. Let $C$ be a collection of subsets of the real line $\mathbb R$, and define
   $$A_\sigma (C) = \bigcap \\{A: C \subset A \text{ and } A \text{ is a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of } R\\}.$$
   a. Prove that $A_\sigma (C)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra, that $C \subset A_\sigma (C)$, and that $A_\sigma (C) \subset A$ for any other $\sigma$-algebra $A$ containing all the sets of $C$.\
   b. Let $O$ be the collection of all finite open intervals in $\mathbb R$, and $F$ the collection of all finite closed intervals in $\mathbb R$.  Show that $A_\sigma (O) = A_\sigma (F)$.

3. Let $(X, A, \mu)$ be a measure space, and suppose $X = \bigcup_n X_n$, where $\\{X\_n\\}\_{n=1}^{\infty}$ is a pairwise disjoint collection of measurable subsets of $X$. Use the Monotone Convergence Theorem and linearity of the integral to prove that, if $f$ is a nonnegative measurable real-valued function on $X$, $$\int_X f\ d\mu = \sum_n \int_{X_n} f\ d\mu.$$

4. Using the Fubini/Tonelli Theorems to justify all steps, evaluate the integral $$\int_0^1 \int_y^1 x^{-3/2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2x}\right) \ dx\ dy.$$
   
5. Let $I$ be the interval $[0,1]$. Let $C(I)$, $C(I\times I)$ denote the spaces of real valued continuous functions on $I$ and $I\times I$, respectively, with the usual supremum norm on these spaces.  Show that the collection of *finite* sums of the form $$f(x,y) = \sum_i \phi_i(x)\psi_i(y) \quad \text{ (where $\phi_i, \psi_i \in C(I)$ for each $i$)}$$  is dense in $C(I\times I)$.

6. Let $m$ be Lebesgue measure on the real line $\mathbb R$, and for each Lebesgue measurable subset $E$ of $\mathbb R$ define $$\mu(E) = \int_E \frac{1}{1+x^2}\ dm(x).$$ Show that $m$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$, and compute the Radon-Nikodym derivative $dm/d\mu$.

### PART B

7. Let $\phi(x,y) = x^2 y$ be defined on the square $S = [0,1]\times [0,1]$ in the plane, and let $m$ be two-dimensional Lebesgue measure on $S$.  Given a Borel subset $E$ of the real line $\mathbb R$, define $\mu(E) = m(\phi^{-1}(E))$.\
   a. Show that $\mu$ is a Borel measure on $\mathbb R$.\
   b. Let $\chi_E$ denote the characteristic function of the set $E$.  Show that $$\int_{\mathbb R}  \chi_E \, d\mu = \int_S  \chi_E \circ \phi\ dm.$$
   c. Evaluate the integral $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t^2\ d\mu(t)$.
  
8. Let $f$ be a real valued and increasing function on the real line $\mathbb R$, such that $f(-\infty)=0$ and $f(\infty)=1$.  Prove that $f$ is absolutely continuous on every closed finite interval if and only if $$\int_{\mathbb R} f'\ dm = 1.$$

9. Let $F$ be a continuous linear functional on the space $L^1[-1,1]$, with the property that $F(f) = 0$ for all *odd* functions $f$ in $L^1[-1,1]$.  Show that there exists an *even* function $\phi$ such that $$F(f) = \int_{-1}^1 f(x) \phi(x)\ dx,$$ for *all* $f\in L^1[-1,1]$. (Hint: One possible approach is to use the fact that any function in $L^p[-1,1]$ is the sum of an odd function and an even function.)

      hypotheses of the monotone convergence theorem are satisfied.  That is,
      for all $x\in X$,
      \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
      \item $0\leq f_1(x) \leq f_2(x) \leq \cdots \leq f(x)$, and
      \item $\limit{n} f_n(x) = f(x)\chi_X(x) = f(x)$.
      \end{enumerate}

        &=  \limit{n} \int_X f_n \, d\mu 
        & & \text{(by definition of $f_n$)}\\
        &= \int_X \limit{n} f_n \, d\mu 
        & &\text{(by the monotone convergence theorem)}\\
        &= \int_X f\, d\mu.

SOLUTION 4
  \ifthenelse{\boolean{includeSolutions}}{\begin{solution}
      By Tonelli's theorem, if $f(x,y)\geq 0$ is measurable and one of the iterated
      integrals $\iint f(x,y)\,dx\,dy$ or $\iint f(x,y)\,dy\,dx$ exists,
      then they both exist and are equal.  Moreover, if one of the iterated integrals is
      finite, then $f(x,y) \in L^1(dx,dy)$.
      Fubini's theorem states: if $f(x,y)\in L^1(dx,dy)$, then the iterated integrals
      exist and are equal.

      Now let $g(x,y) = x^{-3/2}\cos(\pi y/2x)$, and apply the Tonelli theorem to
      the non-negative measurable function $|g(x,y)|$ as follows:
      \[
      \int_0^1 \int_0^x |g(x,y)|\,dy\,dx = 
      \int_0^1 \int_0^x |x|^{-3/2} \left|\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2x}\right)\right|\,dy\,dx
      \leq \int_0^1 \int_0^x x^{-3/2}\cdot 1 \,dy\,dx
      = \int_0^1 x^{-1/2} \,dx = 2.
      \]
      Thus one of the iterated integrals of $|g(x,y)|$ is finite which, by the
      Tonelli theorem, implies $g(x,y)\in L^1(dx,dy)$.  Therefore, the Fubini theorem 
      applies to $g(x,y)$, and gives the first of the following equalities:
      \setlength{\arraycolsep}{0.0em}
      \begin{eqnarray*}
        \int_0^1 \int_y^1 x^{-3/2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2x}\right)\,dx\,dy 
        &{}={}& \int_0^1 \int_0^x x^{-3/2}\cos\left(\frac{\pi y}{2x}\right)\,dy\,dx \\[4pt]
        &{}={}& \int_0^1 x^{-3/2} \cdot \frac{2x}{\pi} 
        \left[\sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{2x}\right)\right]_{y=0}^{y=x} \, dx \\[4pt]
        &{}={}&  \int_0^1 \frac{2}{\pi}x^{-1/2}\, dx \\[4pt]
        &{}={}& \frac{2}{\pi} \left[2 x^{1/2}\right]_{x=0}^{x=1} \\[4pt]
        &{}={}& \frac{4}{\pi}.
      \end{eqnarray*}
      \qed
    \end{solution}  }{}

SOLUTION 6.
\ifthenelse{\boolean{includeSolutions}}{\begin{solution}
      Obviously both measures are non-negative.  We must first prove $m\ll \mu$.  To this end,
      suppose $m(E) >0$, where $E\in \sigM$, the $\sigma$-algebra of Lebesgue measurable
      sets. Then, if we can show $\mu(E)>0$, this will establish that the implication 
      $\mu(E)=0 \Rightarrow m(E)=0$ holds for all $E\in \sigM$; \ie $m\ll\mu$.

      For $n = 1, 2, \ldots$, define
      \[
      A_n = \left\{x\in \R: \frac{1}{n+1} < \frac{1}{1+x^2} \leq \frac{1}{n}\right\}.
      \]
      Then $A_i\cap A_j = \emptyset$ for all $i\neq j$ in $\N$, and, for all $n = 1, 2, \ldots$,
      \[
      \mu(A_n) \geq \frac{1}{n+1} m(A_n).
      \]
      Also, $\R = \cup A_n$, since $0< \frac{1}{1+x^2} \leq 1$ holds for all $x\in \R$. 
      Therefore,
      \[
      \mu(E) = 
      \mu(E\cap (\cup_n A_n)) = 
      \mu(\cup_n (A_n \cap E)) = 
      \sum_n \mu(A_n \cap E).
      \]
      The last equality might need a bit of justification: Since $f(x) = \frac{1}{1+x^2}$
      is continuous, hence measurable, the sets $\{A_n\}$ are measurable.  Therefore, the
      last equality holds by countable additivity of disjoint measurable sets.

      Now note that $m(E) = \sum m(A_n\cap E) >0$ implies the existence of an $n\in \N$
      such that $m(A_n \cap E) >0$.  Therefore, 
      \[
      \mu(E) \geq \mu(A_n\cap E) \geq \frac{1}{n+1} m(A_n\cap E) > 0,
      \]
      which proves that $m\ll \mu$.
      \index{Radon-Nikodym!problems}
      By the Radon-Nikodym theorem~(\ref{cor:radon-nikodym}),
      there is a unique $h\in L^1(\mu)$ such that
      \[
      m(E) = \int h \, d\mu, \quad \text{ and } \quad 
      \int f\, dm  = \int f h \, d\mu \quad  \forall \, f\in L^1(m).
      \]
      In particular, if $E\in \sigM$ and $f(x) = \frac{1}{1+x^2} \chi_E$, then
      \[
      \mu(E) = \int_E \frac{1}{1+x^2} \, dm(x) = \int_E \frac{h(x)}{1+x^2}\, d\mu(x).
      \]
      That is, $\int_E \, d\mu = \int_E \frac{h(x)}{1+x^2}\, d\mu(x)$ holds for all
      measurable sets $E$, which implies\footnote{Recall the standard result: if $f$ and
        $g$ are integrable functions such that $\int_E f = \int_E g$ holds for all measurable
        sets $E$, then $f = g$, $\mu$-a.e.\\This is an exam problem, but I can't remember
        on which exam it appears.  When I come across it again I'll put a cross reference
        here.} that, $\frac{h(x)}{1+x^2} = 1$ holds  
      for $\mu$-almost every $x\in \R$.  Therefore,
      \[
      \frac{dm}{d\mu}(x) = h(x) = 1+x^2.
      \]
      One final note: $h$ is uniquely defined only up to an equivalence class of functions
      that are equal to $1+x^2$, $\mu$-a.e. 
      \qed
    \end{solution}  }{}


SOLUTION 8.
      \footnotetext{
        I have worked this problem a number of times, and what follows is
        the clearest and most instructive proof I've come up with.  It's by no means
        the shortest, most elegant solution, and probably not the type of
        detailed answer one should give on an actual exam.  However, some
        of the facts that I prove in detail have appeared as separate
        questions on other exams, so the proofs are worth knowing.}
      First note that $f$ is increasing, so $f'$ exists for a.e.~$x\in \R$, 
      and $f'(x) \geq 0$ wherever $f'$ exists.  Also, $f'$ is measurable.  To
      see this, define
      \[
      g(x) = \limsup_{n\rightarrow \infty} \, [f(x+1/n)-f(x)] \, n.
      \]
      As a lim sup of a sequence of measurable functions, $g$
      is measurable (Rudin~\cite{Rudin:1987}, theorem 1.14?).  
      Let $E$ be the set on which $f'$ exists.  Then
      $m(\R\setminus E)=0$, and $f'=g$ on $E$ (by the definition of derivative),
      % .  Thus $g$ is measurable and $f'=g$ a.e.  Therefore, 
      so $f'$ is measurable.\\
      \\
      ($\Leftarrow$) \hspace{1mm} Suppose $\int_\R f' dm =1$.  We must show $f\in AC[a,b]$
      for all $-\infty < a < b < \infty$.  First, check that $f' \in
      L^1(\R)$, since  
      \[ 
      1=\int_\R f' dm  = \int_{\R\setminus E} f' dm + \int_{E} f' dm =
      \int_{E} f' dm,
      \]
      % The last equality holds because $m(\R\setminus E) = 0$,
      and, since $f$ is increasing, $f'\geq 0$ on $E$, so
      \[ 
      \int_\R |f'| dm  = \int_{\R\setminus E} |f'| dm + \int_{E} |f'| dm =
      \int_{E} |f'| dm =  \int_{E} f' dm = 1.
      \]
      Thus, $f' \in L^1(\R)$ as claimed.  A couple of lemmas will be
      needed to complete the $\Leftarrow$ direction of the proof.  The first
      is proved in the appendix (sec.~\ref{sec:miscellaneous-proofs}), while the second
      can be found in Royden~\cite{Royden:1988} on page~100.  

      \begin{lemma}\label{lemma:8-1}  
        Let $f:\R\rightarrow \R$ be a function.  If $f$ is
        differentiable on $[a,b]$, $f' \in L^1([a,b])$, and
        $\int_a^x f'(t) dt = f(x) - f(a)$ for $a\leq x \leq b$, then
        $f\in AC[a,b]$.
      \end{lemma}
      The converse of this lemma is also true.\footnote{See Folland~\cite{Folland:1999}
        for a nice, concise treatment of 
        \index{fundamental theorem of calculus}
        the fundamental theorem of calculus for Lebesgue
        integration.} 
      \begin{lemma}\label{lemma:8-2}
        If $f: \R \rightarrow \R$ is increasing and $f' \in L^1([a,b])$, then
        $\int_a^x f'(t) dt \leq f(x) - f(a)$.
      \end{lemma}

      To finish the $\Leftarrow$ direction of the proof, by
      lemma~\ref{lemma:8-1}, it suffices to show that 
      $\int_\R f' dm =1$ implies $\int_a^b f'(t) dt = f(b) - f(a)$ holds
      for all $-\infty < a < b < \infty$. By lemma~\ref{lemma:8-2}, we 
      have $\int_a^b f'(t) dt \leq f(b) - f(a)$, so we need
      only show that strict inequality cannot hold.  Suppose, by way of
      contradiction, that $\int_a^b f'(t) dt < f(b) - f(a)$ holds for some 
      $-\infty < a < b < \infty$.  Then,
      % By this lemma, it suffices to show that 
      % $\int_a^x f'(t) dt < f(x) - f(a)$ cannot hold for any
      % $-\infty < a < b < \infty$. Suppose it does hold
      \setlength{\arraycolsep}{0.0em}
      \begin{eqnarray*} %\label{eq:3}
        1 &{}={}& \int_\R f' dm = \int_{-\infty}^a f' dm + \int_a^b f' dm +
        \int_b^\infty f' dm \nonumber\\ 
        &{}<{}& [f(a)-f(-\infty)]+[f(b)-f(a)]+[f(\infty)-f(b)]\nonumber\\
        &{}={}& f(\infty)-f(-\infty) = 1.
      \end{eqnarray*}
      This contradiction proves that $\int_\R f' dm =1$ implies $\int_a^b
      f'(t) dt = f(b) - f(a)$ holds for all $-\infty < a < b < \infty$, as
      desired. \\
      \\
      ($\Rightarrow$)\hspace{1mm} Now assume $f\in AC[a,b]$ for all 
      $-\infty<a<b<\infty$. We must show 
      $\int_\R f' dm =1$.  By assumption $f(\infty)-f(-\infty) = 1$, so this
      is equivalent to showing
      \[
      \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty}\int_{-x}^x f'(t) dm(t) = 
      \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty}[f(x)-f(-x)].
      \]
      Let $x\in \R$, $x>0$, and $f\in AC[-x,x]$.  Then we claim
      $f(x)-f(-x) = \int_{-x}^x f' dm$.  Assuming the claim is true (see
      Royden~\cite{Royden:1988}, p.~110 for the proof), we have
      \[
      1 =     \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty}[f(x)-f(-x)] = 
      \lim_{x\rightarrow \infty}\int_{-x}^x f'(t) dm(t) = \int_\R f' dm.
      \]
      \qed
    \end{solution}  }{}

SOLUTION 9
\ifthenelse{\boolean{includeSolutions}}{\begin{solution}
      Since $F\in L^1[-1,1]^*$, then by the Riesz representation theorem\footnote{See
        problem~\ref{item:2001-3-1} of section~\ref{sec:2001-november-26}.} there is a unique
      $h\in L^\infty[-1,1]$ such that  
      \[
      F(f) = \int^1_{-1} f(x) h(x) \, dx \qquad (\forall f\in L^1[-1,1])
      \]
      Now (using the hint) write $h = \phi + \psi$, where $\phi$ and $\psi$ are the even
      and odd functions
      \[
      \phi(x) = \frac{h(x) + h(-x)}{2} \quad \text{ and } \quad
      \psi(x) = \frac{h(x) - h(-x)}{2}.
      \]
      Similarly, let $f = f_e + f_o$ be the decomposition of $f$ into a sum of even and odd
      functions.  Then, by linearity of $F$, and since $F(f_o) = 0$ by hypothesis,
      \[
      F(f) = F(f_e) + F(f_o) = F(f_e) = \int_{-1}^1 f_e h = \int_{-1}^1 f_e \phi+
      \int_{-1}^1 f_e \psi.
      \]
      Now note that $f_e \psi$ is an odd function (since it's an even times an odd), so 
      $\int_{-1}^1 f_e \psi = 0$, since
      $[-1,1]$ is symmetric.  Similarly, $\int_{-1}^1 f_o \phi = 0$.
      Therefore,
      \[
      F(f) = F(f_e) = \int_{-1}^1 f_e \phi = \int_{-1}^1 f_e \phi+ \int_{-1}^1 f_o \phi 
      = \int_{-1}^1 (f_e+f_o) \phi = \int_{-1}^1 f \phi.
      \]
      \qed
      \index{Riesz representation theorem!applied|)}
    \end{solution}  }{}
\end{enumerate}
